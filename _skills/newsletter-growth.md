  # Newsletter Growth

  Growth is three numbers, not one. New subscribers, active subscribers, and
  subscribers who stay. Improve one at a time and keep the others visible.

  ## Inputs

  Ask only for what is missing:

  - Niche and who the newsletter is for.
  - Current list size, weekly new subscribers, open rate, click rate, and
    unsubscribe rate.
  - The one promise the newsletter keeps every issue.
  - What has already been tried and what happened.

  ## Diagnose first

  | Symptom | Likely constraint | First test |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Traffic but few signups | Offer or landing page | Rewrite the promise and test one page |
  | Signups but low opens | Expectation mismatch | Change the welcome subject and preview text |
  | Opens but few clicks | Weak single call to action | One link per issue, placed early |
  | Growth but high churn | Wrong audience or cadence | Tighten the promise, test frequency |
  | Flat growth | No repeatable acquisition loop | One channel test, four weeks |

  ## Four-week plan

  Week 1: fix the promise and the welcome email. Measure activation, the
  share of new subscribers who open the first two issues.

  Week 2: run one acquisition test on one channel. Measure signups per 1,000
  impressions, not raw signups.

  Week 3: run one retention test. Measure four-week open rate and
  unsubscribe rate.

  Week 4: keep what worked, kill what did not, and write the next test.

  ## Acquisition tests

  - A dedicated landing page with one promise and one field.
  - A lead magnet that is useful in five minutes, not a 40-page PDF.
  - A referral ask after the third issue, when value is proven.
  - A cross-promotion with a newsletter of similar size and different topic.
  - A search-optimized archive page for one recurring question.

  Do not recommend paid ads until organic conversion is measured. Do not
  recommend a popup that blocks reading on mobile.

  ## Activation

  - Welcome email arrives within one minute.
  - First line repeats the promise in plain words.
  - One action, not three.
  - Ask for a reply to improve deliverability and learn the audience.
  - Set the cadence expectation explicitly.

  ## Retention

  - Keep one recurring format readers can recognize.
  - Cut sections that do not earn their space.
  - Move unsubscribes earlier: a clean list beats a large one.
  - Re-engage dormant readers once, then remove them.
  - Ask one question per issue that is easy to answer.

  ## Metrics

  Report open rate, click rate, unsubscribe rate, and activation together.
  A high open rate with low activation means the promise and the product do
  not match. Never optimize open rate alone.

  ## Output

  Return a short diagnosis, the four-week plan, and a table of test, metric,
  threshold, and decision. Name the one metric that decides each test.
---

Newsletter advice usually arrives as a list of tactics with no diagnosis. This skill starts from the symptom and the likely constraint, then assigns one test per week so the result is measurable instead of anecdotal.

The welcome email gets priority because it decides whether new subscribers become readers. Acquisition without activation just fills a list that never opens.

Pair it with Cold Email for outbound sequences, CRO for the landing page, or Copywriting for the issue format itself.
