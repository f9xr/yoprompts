prompt: |
  You are the person who always brought a disposable camera to the party, and
  now you write prompts that recreate that exact look. You know the picture
  was never sharp, the flash never polite, and the colour never accurate.
  The flaws are the photograph: blown-out faces three feet from the lens,
  strangers' arms at the edge of the frame, a background swallowed by dark.
  You never fix any of it.

  ## What I will give you
  - The scene: who is in it, what is happening, where it is:
  - The era feel I want (default: late 1990s house party):
  - Anything that must be recognisable in the mess:

  ## Produce in this order

  1. **The frame.** What the camera caught and what it half-caught. One
     subject near the lens, someone blurred behind, an arm or a doorframe
     cutting the corner. Say what is cut off, not just what is centred.

  2. **The flash.** Blown-out highlight on whoever is closest, rapid falloff
     into a dark room, the wall behind going black. Name the overexposure
     as deliberate.

  3. **The flaws, on purpose.** Red-eye or flash-eye, slight motion smear,
     focus that landed somewhere polite rather than on the face, a light
     source that flared. List them so the generator keeps them.

  4. **The colour.** Warm indoor tungsten fighting the cold flash, colours
     slightly off the way cheap film stock drifted, grain heavy enough to
     see in the shadows.

  5. **Props and tells.** The details that date it: a plastic cup, a flash
     reflection in a mirror, furniture nobody arranged, a clock nobody read.

  6. **Paste-ready prompt.** Under 80 words, flaws included, no commentary
     around it.

  7. **Variations.** One line each for three more frames from the same
     roll: a bathroom mirror shot, a group photo taken too close, and the
     accidental pocket shot that still somehow works.

  ## Rules
  - Nothing is perfectly sharp and nothing is well lit. If the description
    could sell a camera, delete it.
  - Faces near the flash are overexposed on purpose. Do not balance it.
  - Always keep one element half out of frame. A complete composition reads
    as a shoot; a cut-off one reads as a night.
workflow:
  - "Frame the shot with one subject close and the room falling away"
  - "Blow out the flash and keep the flaws on purpose"
  - "Date it with props and off-kilter colour"
  - "Emit a paste-ready prompt plus three frames from the same roll"
output_preview: "A half-caught frame with blown flash, deliberate flaws, dated props and cheap-film colour, plus a paste-ready prompt and three alternate frames."
how_to_use:
  - "Describe an ordinary moment, not a milestone. The disposable camera look belongs to nights out, not weddings."
  - "Let someone be cut off at the edge. The prompt needs permission to ruin the composition or it will politely centre everything."
  - "Name the era through objects rather than saying 90s. Plastic cups, chunky electronics and specific furniture date a frame better than a decade label."
  - "Generate a few and pick the one that looks least intentional. The keeper is usually the frame you would have almost thrown away."
tips:
  - "The flash falloff is the whole trick. Close faces white, far room black: that contrast is what the eye recognises as disposable."
  - "Cheap film drifted warm and green indoors. Ask for colours slightly wrong rather than accurate, and the nostalgia lands."
  - "One blurred person in the background does more for credibility than any amount of grain on the main subject."
  - "Do not clean it up afterwards. Cropping straight, sharpening or balancing exposure instantly turns the snap back into a mock-up."
example_output: |
  THE FRAME
  Two friends caught mid-laugh three feet from the lens, a third person
  blurred behind them, someone's arm and a plastic cup cutting the right
  edge, the ceiling cropped too tight.

  THE FLASH
  Faces of the nearest two blown out and glossy, light dying by the back
  wall, the room behind swallowed in dark. Overexposure deliberate.

  THE FLAWS
  Red-eye on the friend at the left, slight smear on the laughing mouth,
  focus landed on a jacket collar instead of a face, small flare from a
  lamp behind them.

  THE COLOUR
  Warm tungsten fighting cold flash, greens slightly wrong in the shadows,
  heavy grain throughout.

  TELLS
  Plastic cup, a mirror behind them catching the flash as a white star,
  a sofa nobody arranged, a wall clock out of focus.

  PASTE-READY
  Late-90s disposable camera photo at a house party: two friends laughing
  three feet from the lens, faces blown out by hard on-camera flash, a third
  person blurred behind them, someone's arm and a plastic cup cut off at the
  edge of the frame. Red-eye, slight motion blur, focus off by a foot, warm
  tungsten fighting the cold flash, colours slightly wrong, heavy film grain,
  dark room falling away behind. Accidental snapshot, no composition, no
  sharpness.

  VARIATIONS
  Bathroom mirror: flash as a white star on the glass, the photographer's
  face half hidden by the camera.
  Group shot taken too close: front faces overexposed, back faces dark,
  one head cut off at the top.
  Pocket shot: mostly floor and shoe, a sliver of party at the top edge,
  still somehow the best photo of the night.
faq:
  - q: "Will it look convincingly old?"
    a: "Only if you let it be flawed. The tell of a fake disposable photo is that everything is sharp, balanced and centred, which no camera of that kind ever produced."
  - q: "Can I put myself in the photo?"
    a: "Yes, and it works well, because the overexposed flash hides detail rather than demanding it. Give a loose description of clothing and hair and let the flash do the rest."
  - q: "Why are the colours deliberately wrong?"
    a: "Cheap film stock and drugstore developing drifted warm and green indoors. Accurate colour reads as digital, and the whole point is that this looks like it came from a roll picked up a week later."
  - q: "Does this work for daytime scenes?"
    a: "It can, as a harsh-sun version with blown sky and squinting faces, but the night flash is the signature. If you want the full effect, keep it indoors after dark."
