Accuracy disclaimer

A good prompt does not make a model truthful. It makes a model more consistently produce the shape of output you wanted. Those are different things, and conflating them is how people end up confidently wrong.

Last updated 4 October 2026

The core limitation

Every prompt on this site improves the form of a response. None of them can guarantee its content is true. Models generate plausible text, and plausible is not the same as correct. They will invent statistics, citations, dates, quotations and job titles with complete confidence, and a prompt that makes output look more professional does nothing to change that.

How prompts here are tested

Be clear about what our testing does and does not establish.

Testing a prompt means running it repeatedly against named models and checking whether it reliably produces:

  • The intended structure and level of detail.
  • Output without invented sources or figures.
  • Consistent behaviour across different inputs.
  • Behaviour that holds up when the input is deliberately thin.

Testing a prompt does not establish:

  • That any factual claim in the output is correct.
  • That it will behave identically on your account, your plan, or a future model version.
  • That it performs safely in a regulated domain.

Models change without notice

Providers release model updates continuously and roll them out gradually. A prompt that worked reliably last month may produce noticeably different output this month, on the same settings. Where a prompt lists the models it was tested against, that list is a timestamped record, not a guarantee.

What you must verify yourself

Treat AI output as a first draft produced by a fast, confident non-expert. Before acting on it, check:

  • Figures and arithmetic. Recompute anything numeric before it reaches a document.
  • Quotations and sources. If the output cites something, open the source. Confirm it says what the output claims.
  • Dates, versions and prices. These drift constantly and are frequently stale in training data.
  • Named people and job titles. Assumptions about someone's role, employer or location are usually invented.
  • Code. Run it. Run the tests. A plausible-looking function can still be wrong.
  • Anything you cannot afford to be wrong about. Check it against a primary source.

High-stakes domains

Prompts touching medical, legal, financial, tax or safety decisions require professional review. An AI system is not a substitute for a licensed practitioner, and nothing generated using a prompt from this site should be treated as professional advice. This applies to research summarisation and code review with real consequences just as much as it does to direct advice.

Bias and limitations of the models themselves

AI models reflect their training data, which contains human assumptions, omissions and prejudice. Asking for output “without bias” does not remove bias from the underlying model. Be aware of this when generating anything about people, particularly groups already underrepresented in that data.

Errors in these pages

We correct factual errors when we find them. If something here is wrong, tell us at hello@f9xr.org and we will fix it. We would rather correct a page than defend it.
