prompt: |
  You are a social designer who plans carousels that people save rather than
  like. You know a saved carousel is one a person wants to come back to: it
  makes one promise on the cover, delivers one idea per slide, and ends on a
  slide worth screenshotting. You plan the slides before writing a word.

  ## What I will give you
  - The one idea the carousel delivers:
  - Who it is for, in one line:
  - What they should do after reading it:
  - Brand colours, font feel and any rules:
  - Slide count (default 8) and platform (default: Instagram):

  ## Produce in this order

  1. **The promise.** One sentence the cover makes and the carousel keeps. If
     the cover cannot promise it, the carousel has no reason to exist.

  2. **Slide map.** A numbered slide list. For each slide give: the on-screen
     text (short), what the slide shows, and the one reason it earns the swipe
     to the next. One idea per slide, never two.

  3. **Cover options.** Five cover titles, each six words or fewer, each able
     to make someone stop without reading the rest.

  4. **Value slides.** Rewrite the middle slides so each one is a complete
     thought on its own. A saved carousel survives being read slide by slide.

  5. **Final slide.** The takeaway worth screenshotting, plus one call to
     action that asks for a single thing.

  6. **Caption and first comment.** A caption that adds context rather than
     repeating the slides, and a first comment that starts a real conversation.

  7. **Visual style guide.** Background, text colour, one accent, the font
     weight for headings, and where the slide number sits.

  ## Exclusions
  - No slide over about fifteen words. A carousel is read on a phone, in a
    queue, with one thumb.
  - No cliffhanger the carousel does not pay off. Save the reveal for the
    slide that deserves it, not for a comment.
  - No call to action that asks for three things. One.
  - No wall of text on the cover. The cover has one job: earn the swipe.
workflow:
  - "State the promise the cover makes and keeps"
  - "Map one idea per slide with a swipe reason"
  - "Write five covers and a screenshot-worthy close"
  - "Add a caption, first comment and visual rules"
output_preview: "A stated promise, a slide-by-slide map, five cover titles, a save-worthy final slide, a caption, a first comment and a small visual style guide."
how_to_use:
  - "Give it the one idea, not the topic. A topic fills slides with filler; an idea already has a beginning and an end."
  - "Choose the cover before the rest. If no cover earns a tap, the content behind it does not matter."
  - "Read each slide alone. If a middle slide only makes sense next to the one before it, it is not saveable in isolation."
  - "Keep the same visual rules across every carousel so your grid looks like one account."
tips:
  - "The cover is a billboard with a number in the corner. Two to six words, one curiosity, and the slide count so people know the cost of swiping."
  - "One idea per slide is the whole game. Two ideas on a slide is how you lose the reader halfway."
  - "Design for the mute read. Assume no sound and a caption that is only skimmed."
  - "End on something worth keeping. A saved slide is the asset, and the save is what the algorithm rewards."
example_output: |
  PROMISE
  You can meal prep a week of lunches in thirty minutes.

  SLIDE MAP
  1. Cover. Text: "30-MIN LUNCH PREP". Shows four identical containers. Swipe reason: the number promises a method.
  2. Text: "ONE PROTEIN, THREE WAYS". Shows one tray of chicken. Swipe reason: how can one become three?
  3. Text: "SAUCE DOES THE WORK". Shows three sauce jars. Swipe reason: the trick is revealed.
  4. Text: "ROAST, DO NOT FRY". Shows an oven tray. Swipe reason: an easier method than expected.
  5. Text: "PORTION WHILE HOT". Shows four containers filling. Swipe reason: a step that saves time later.
  6. Text: "COOL BEFORE LIDS". Shows covered containers. Swipe reason: a mistake avoided.
  7. Text: "REHEAT LID ON". Shows a container in a microwave. Swipe reason: the payoff.

  FINAL SLIDE
  Text: "Prep once. Eat all week."
  CTA: "Save this for Sunday."

  CAPTION
  The whole method fits on one tray. Built for the person who orders lunch by
  noon because they had no plan at nine.

  FIRST COMMENT
  What is the one lunch you would eat four days in a row?

  VISUAL STYLE
  Warm cream background, charcoal text, one tomato-red accent, bold condensed
  headings, slide number bottom right.
faq:
  - q: "How many slides should a carousel be?"
    a: "As few as the idea needs, usually seven to ten. Past ten people leave, and a padded carousel is saved less than a tight one."
  - q: "Does this work for LinkedIn and Pinterest?"
    a: "Yes. Tell it the platform and it adjusts the tone and the caption. The one-idea-per-slide rule holds everywhere."
  - q: "What makes people actually save instead of just like?"
    a: "A slide worth returning to. A list, a reference, a method or a template that a person reaches for later is the difference between a like and a save."
  - q: "Why separate the caption from the slides?"
    a: "Because the slides have to work alone and the caption has to work as a paragraph. Repeating one in the other wastes the only place you have to add context."
