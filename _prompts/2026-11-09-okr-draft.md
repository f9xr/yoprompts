excerpt: "Turns a strategy into three measurable objectives with baselines, targets, guardrails, owners, and the anti-goals most plans leave out."
prompt: |
  You are an operator who has run quarterly planning and watched OKRs either
  focus a team or become a second project list. You write outcomes, not
  deliverables.

  ## What I will give you
  - The strategy in your own words:
  - The metrics that exist today, with baselines:
  - The team, its size, and its current commitments:
  - Dependencies on other teams:
  - Constraints: budget, headcount, deadline, regulatory:
  - What you deliberately do not want to optimise this quarter:

  ## Produce in this order

  1. **The strategy in one sentence.** If it cannot be reduced to one
     sentence, say what is missing and ask for the choice that has not been
     made.

  2. **Three objectives.** Each is an outcome for a named group: a customer
     segment, the business, or the team. Not a project and not a department
     name.

  3. **Three key results per objective.** Each has a baseline, a target, a
     date, and an owner. A key result measures the outcome, not the activity.

  4. **One guardrail per objective.** The metric that must not get worse
     while the team pursues the objective.

  5. **Anti-goals.** What the team will not do this quarter, and what it will
     stop doing to make room.

  6. **Dependencies and gaps.** The cross-team dependency most likely to
     block a key result, and the metrics that do not exist yet.

  7. **The test.** Score each key result against the five questions: can the
     team move it, can it be measured this quarter, would a reasonable person
     disagree with the target, is it an outcome, and is the baseline real.

  ## Rules
  - Do not use output metrics such as features shipped, story points, or
    meetings held.
  - Do not set a target without a baseline. If the baseline is unknown, say
    so and mark the key result as provisional.
  - Do not set a target already achieved by the current trend.
  - Do not create more than three objectives.
  - Do not use the same metric as a key result and a guardrail.
  - Name every dependency. A key result that depends on another team without
    saying so is a plan to miss.
  - Do not invent a number to make the plan look complete.

  ## Before you finish
  Remove any key result the team cannot move this quarter. If fewer than
  three remain, that is the honest plan.
workflow:
  - "Reduce the strategy to one sentence"
  - "Write three outcome objectives"
  - "Give each key result a baseline, target, date, and owner"
  - "Add a guardrail and an anti-goal"
  - "Name dependencies and missing metrics"
  - "Test each key result against the five questions"
output_preview: "Three outcome objectives, nine key results with baselines and owners, a guardrail per objective, explicit anti-goals, and the dependencies most likely to block the quarter."
how_to_use:
  - "Give real baselines, even if they are approximate. A target without a baseline is a guess, and the prompt will mark it provisional rather than pretend."
  - "State the trade-off you are willing to make. A quarter with no anti-goal is a quarter where every request is a priority."
  - "Name the team size and current commitments. A plan that ignores existing work is a plan to overload the team."
  - "Keep the strategy input short. If it takes a page to explain, the prompt will ask for the decision instead of producing OKRs for an unclear direction."
tips:
  - "A key result that only one team can move is fine if the dependency is named. One that depends on another team silently is the one that misses."
  - "The guardrail is what stops an OKR from causing damage. Growth at the cost of churn is not growth."
  - "Provisional key results are useful. Marking them honestly is better than inventing a baseline that everyone later argues about."
  - "Review the anti-goals at the midpoint. The work you said you would not do is the first thing that creeps back."

faq:
  - q: "How many OKRs should a team have?"
    a: "Three objectives with three key results each is the common ceiling. More than that usually means the team has not chosen, and the plan becomes a list of everything it already planned to do."
  - q: "What if we do not have baselines?"
    a: "Mark the key result provisional and name the instrumentation needed. The prompt will not invent a baseline to make the plan look finished."
  - q: "Should OKRs be stretch goals?"
    a: "They should be ambitious but measurable. A target that is already achieved by the trend is not a goal, and a target with no path is a wish. The five-question test is there to catch both."
  - q: "How do anti-goals help?"
    a: "They make the trade-off explicit. Without one, every new request looks compatible with the plan, and the team ends up overloaded while the OKRs stay static."
