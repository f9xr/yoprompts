excerpt: "Writes a pitch that quotes the show back at the host, names the episode you would slot between, and offers three angles instead of a biography."
prompt: |
  You are a podcast booker who receives forty pitches a week and books four.
  You know that almost every pitch fails on the same three things: it is about
  the guest rather than the show, it does not prove the guest has listened, and
  it offers a topic rather than an episode.

  ## What I will give you
  - The show, and two recent episodes with what they covered:
  - Who I am, in one sentence, with the thing I have actually done:
  - The argument I want to make on air, including who disagrees with it:
  - The audience the host is trying to serve:

  ## What I want back

  1. **Three episode angles**, each stated as a title a listener would click.
     For each: the claim, the story or data I would bring, and the episode of
     theirs it continues. If an angle duplicates something they covered in the
     last six months, cut it and say why.

  2. **The pitch email**, under 150 words. Rules: open with the specific
     episode and the moment in it I am responding to; state the argument in
     one sentence; give one line of credential, not a paragraph; close with an
     offer, not a request for their time.

  3. **A subject line set** of five, each under fifty characters, none using
     "guest", "podcast guest", or "quick question".

  4. **A three-sentence bio** for the show notes, written in third person,
     with one concrete number and no adjectives about my own style.

  5. **What would make the host say no**, and the sentence in my pitch that
     creates the risk.

  ## Rules
  - No flattery openers. "Love the show" is the single most common phrase in
    a booker's inbox and it is read as a template.
  - Do not claim audience numbers I did not give you.
  - If my credentials do not support the angle, say which angle is a stretch
    and what evidence would fix it.
workflow:

output_preview: "Three episode angles tied to real recent episodes, a sub-150-word pitch, five short subject lines, a show-notes bio, and the line most likely to sink it."
how_to_use:
  - "Paste actual episode titles and what they argued. A pitch that cannot name a moment from the show reads as a mail merge."
  - "Give the host's audience, not yours. The booker is choosing an episode for their listeners, and your audience size only matters if it is larger than theirs."
  - "Send one angle per email, not three. Three reads as a menu and makes the host do the choosing."
  - "Follow up once, seven days later, with a new piece of evidence rather than a reminder."
tips:
  - "Ask it for the no-risk analysis first and paste that into your own notes. It usually identifies the same objection the host would raise, which is the thing to pre-empt in the next pitch."
  - "If you have no public work, swap the credential line for a specific result you can describe in one sentence. Vague authority is worse than a small concrete number."
  - "Run the three angles against the show's last ten episodes, not two. Repeat guests and resurfaced topics are the fastest no."
  - "Keep a list of every episode you reference. It becomes a real targeting list, and it stops you pitching the same show twice with the same angle."
example_output: |
  SUBJECT: The counter-argument to your episode 214
  (46 characters)

  PITCH (128 words)
  Your episode 214 argued that pricing pages should lead with the cheapest
  plan. I have moved four SaaS companies off that structure and the ones that
  switched to highest-plan-first saw trial quality improve while total
  signups dropped. The trade is real, though, and worth arguing about.
  The episode I would make: "The pricing page that deliberately converts
  fewer people." I would bring the before-and-after numbers from two of
  those moves, including the one where it went badly.
  I run pricing research at [company] and have shipped 31 pricing changes in
  four years. Happy to send the data first if that is easier to judge than a
  promise.

  NO-RISK: "31 pricing changes" invites "so you broke 31 pages?" Have the
  two failures ready in the first minute.
faq:
  - q: "How long should a pitch be?"
    a: "Long enough to prove you listened, short enough to read on a phone between meetings. Under 150 words with one specific reference is the reliable shape; the reference is what buys the second paragraph."
  - q: "Should I attach a media kit?"
    a: "No, not in the first email. Attachments from strangers go unopened, and a media kit answers a question the host has not asked yet. Offer it after they reply."
  - q: "What if the show has a booking form?"
    a: "Use the form and paste the same pitch. The form is a filter, not a different conversation, and the episode-specific detail is what gets it past the filter."
  - q: "Is it worth pitching shows with a small audience?"
    a: "Often yes. Small shows in a narrow niche book faster, ask better questions, and their listeners are the ones who buy. Pitch the show whose audience you want, not the biggest logo."
