Learn to prompt properly once

Most disappointing AI output is not a model failure. It is an underspecified request. These five techniques fix the large majority of it — and everything else on this site is downstream of them.

The five basics

If you read nothing else on this page, read this section. In rough order of how much they improve output.

1. Give it a role, not a task

“Write me a product description” gets you marketing filler, because you have not said who is writing or for whom.

“You are a direct-response copywriter who has shipped landing pages for B2B SaaS. Write a product description for…” narrows vocabulary, register and what counts as a good example. One sentence, and it is the highest-leverage sentence in the whole prompt.

2. Forbid invention

Models will happily invent a statistic, a citation, a deadline or a person's job title to fill a gap you left. Then you spend longer fact-checking than you would have spent writing.

Close every prompt with an explicit prohibition:

Never invent a source, a number, or a date. If you need one and I did not give it to you, write [NEEDED] and tell me exactly what you need.

Giving the model a legal way to decline beats telling it not to guess. [NEEDED] becomes a to-do list; a fabricated citation becomes your problem.

3. Say what "done" looks like

Vague output is usually an underspecified deliverable. Name the shape: a numbered list, a JSON object with these keys, a 200-word paragraph with no preamble, a diff.

Every prompt in this library states its output format for exactly this reason.

4. Put the constraints last

Models weight recent instructions heavily. Structure a long prompt as role, then task, then hard rules — and keep the rules at the bottom where they are still in focus when generation starts.

5. Make it degrade gracefully

Tell it what to do when your input is too thin:

If what I gave you is not enough to produce section 3, say so, list the one thing you most need, and stop. Do not pad.

This single line removes most of the filler that makes AI output feel hollow.

Writing your own

A prompt worth keeping follows a shape. Context, task, format, constraints, and a fallback. You can submit one to the library if you have built something better than what is already here — genuinely better, not just different.
