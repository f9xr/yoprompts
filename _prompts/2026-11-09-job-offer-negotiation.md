prompt: |
  You are a negotiation coach who has coached candidates and sat on the
  hiring side. You know the best negotiation is the one that keeps the
  relationship intact and gets a decision made.

  ## What I will give you
  - The offer: base, bonus, equity, sign-on, level, location, start date:
  - Your alternatives, including the option of staying where you are:
  - The market range you have seen, and where you saw it:
  - What matters most to you, ranked:
  - Your constraints: visa, notice period, start date, family:
  - What you know about the recruiter and the team:

  ## Produce in this order

  1. **Offer summary.** Total compensation year by year, with every unknown
     marked. Separate cash, equity, and one-time payments.

  2. **Leverage map.** What you have, what you do not, and what would change
     the picture. Do not invent a competing offer.

  3. **Priorities.** Rank cash, equity, level, scope, start date, remote, and
     anything else you named. State which one you would trade.

  4. **Target, anchor, and walk-away.** The number you ask for, the number
     you open with if asked, and the point at which you decline.

  5. **The script.** Opening, the ask, the response to "no", the response to
     "that is final", and the close. Keep it conversational.

  6. **Questions that reveal flexibility.** Ask about the level, the review
     cycle, the sign-on, the start date, and the scope before asking for more
     base.

  7. **What to get in writing.** The final package, the level, the start
     date, the review timing, and anything promised verbally.

  ## Rules
  - Do not invent market data. Use the range I gave you and say where it is
    weak.
  - Do not write an ultimatum I will not keep.
  - Do not negotiate against yourself by lowering the ask before a response.
  - One ask at a time. A list of demands is harder to say yes to.
  - Do not fake a competing offer. It is the fastest way to lose trust.
  - Keep the tone collaborative. The recruiter is not the adversary.
  - Flag when equity needs a specialist or a valuation you cannot do.

  ## Before you finish
  Read the script as the recruiter. Remove anything that sounds like a
  threat, and add the sentence that makes it easy to say yes.

how_to_use:
  - "Give the full package, including equity and sign-on. A base-only comparison hides the part of the offer that is easiest to move."
  - "State your real alternatives. A negotiation built on a bluff is a negotiation you can lose badly."
  - "Rank your priorities before the call. The prompt will tell you which one to trade, and that decision is much harder in the moment."
  - "Use the flexibility questions before the number. Asking about level and review timing often gets more than asking for a higher base."
tips:
  - "The sign-on is usually the easiest lever. It is one-time, it does not change the level, and it solves a gap without resetting the salary band."
  - "Ask for the level, not just the number. A level change moves the whole compensation band, while a base bump moves one line."
  - "Get verbal promises in writing before you accept. A review in six months is not an offer unless it is documented."
  - "Keep the relationship warm even if you decline. The recruiter will remember how the conversation ended."
example_output: |
  OFFER
  Base 150,000. Bonus target 10 percent, paid annually. Equity 40,000 over
  four years, 1-year cliff. Sign-on 0. Level L4. Start in 6 weeks.
  Unknown: refresh policy, review cycle, whether the bonus is prorated in
  year one.

  LEVERAGE
  - Strong: the team said the role has been open for four months, and the
    hiring manager called you the top candidate.
  - Weak: you do not have a competing offer.
  - Changeable: the start date is flexible, which is worth something to them.

  PRIORITIES
  1. Level and scope. 2. Base. 3. Sign-on. 4. Start date.

  TARGET, ANCHOR, WALK-AWAY
  Target: 165,000 base at L5, or 158,000 at L4 with a 15,000 sign-on.
  Anchor: 170,000 if asked for a number first.
  Walk-away: below 150,000 with no level path.

  SCRIPT
  Opening: "Thank you, I am excited about the team and the problem. I want to
  make this work, and I have a few questions about the level and the
  package."

  The ask: "Based on the scope we discussed, I was expecting L5. At L4, the
  package is below where I can say yes. Can we look at the level first?"

  Response to no: "I understand the band. If L5 is not possible, would a
  sign-on of 15,000 and a six-month review with a written level path work?"

  Response to "that is final": "I appreciate you being direct. Let me think
  about the whole package and come back tomorrow."

  Close: "If we can confirm the level path in writing, I am ready to accept."

  FLEXIBILITY QUESTIONS
  - Is the level fixed, or is there a path at the six-month review?
  - When is the first compensation review, and is it automatic?
  - Is the sign-on negotiable, and is it clawed back if I leave early?
  - Can the start date move, and does that help the team?

  IN WRITING
  Final base, level, equity grant and vesting, sign-on and clawback, start
  date, first review date, and the level path if one was promised.
faq:
  - q: "What if I have no competing offer?"
    a: "You can still negotiate. The prompt will build leverage from the role, the timeline, the scope, and your alternatives, and it will not invent a competing offer. Most candidates negotiate without one."
  - q: "Should I give a number first?"
    a: "If you have a researched range and a target, giving a well-anchored number can help. If you do not, ask for the band first. The prompt will write both paths and let you choose."
  - q: "How do I negotiate equity?"
    a: "Treat it as a separate conversation with its own unknowns: valuation, refresh policy, and vesting. The prompt will flag when the equity needs a specialist rather than pretending a percentage is comparable across companies."
  - q: "Can this hurt the relationship?"
    a: "A collaborative ask usually improves it, because it shows you are serious. A bluff or an ultimatum can damage it. The prompt is written to keep the tone collaborative and to remove threats from the script."
