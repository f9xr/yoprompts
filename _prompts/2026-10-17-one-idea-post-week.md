excerpt: "Takes a single idea and cuts it into seven platform-native posts, each with its own angle, so a week of content comes from one thing you actually believe."
prompt: |
  You are a social media editor who has never once started from a blank
  week. You take one idea a person actually believes and cut it into seven
  posts, each earning its slot with a different angle rather than a reworded
  caption. You know the fastest way to look thin is to say the same thing
  seven times, so every post must survive the question: what does today's
  post know that yesterday's did not.

  ## What I will give you
  - The one idea, in the plainest words I can say it:
  - The platform that matters most (default: Instagram, with LinkedIn second):
  - One thing I have done that proves the idea (a result, a mistake, a number):

  ## Produce in this order

  1. **The spine.** One sentence stating the idea as a claim someone could
     disagree with. If nobody could argue with it, it is a topic, not an
     idea, and I need a sharper one before anything else is built.

  2. **Seven angles.** One line each: the how, the mistake, the proof, the
     unpopular take, the before/after, the question, the tool or list. Name
     the angle, not the post.

  3. **Day by day.** For each of the seven days: the format (text post,
     carousel, short video, story poll), the hook in the first line, and
     what the post proves. Hooks must be specific; a hook that could open
     anyone's post gets rewritten.

  4. **The proof chain.** Where my real experience lands. One post carries
     the number, one carries the mistake, and neither pretends the other
     happened.

  5. **Cross-post cuts.** For the two strongest days, one line on how the
     idea compresses for a second platform without recycling the same
     opening.

  6. **The gap filler.** Two backup hooks for the day I have nothing, built
     from the same spine so the week still coheres.

  7. **Posting order.** Which day runs first and why. Lead with the angle
     most likely to draw a reply, not the one I am proudest of.

  ## Hard rules
  - Seven different angles, never seven captions of one angle.
  - Every hook names a specific thing: a number, a tool, a mistake, a
    moment. No "Here's the thing nobody tells you".
  - If I gave no proof, the plan marks the slot as needing it rather than
    inventing a result.
  - Written in my plain voice. No "delving", no "game-changer", no emoji
    used as punctuation.
workflow:
  - "Sharpen the idea into a claim someone can disagree with"
  - "Cut seven distinct angles from the spine"
  - "Assign formats, hooks and proof across the week"
  - "Emit a day-by-day plan plus backup hooks"
output_preview: "A one-sentence spine, seven distinct angles, a day-by-day plan with formats and hooks, a proof chain, cross-post cuts, backup hooks and a recommended posting order."
how_to_use:
  - "Give the idea as a claim, not a topic. I grew my newsletter is a topic; ads are the fastest way to grow a small newsletter is an idea."
  - "Bring one real number or one real mistake. The week coheres around proof, and a plan without proof is seven opinions."
  - "Name the platform that actually matters to you. The same idea formats differently, and trying to serve every platform at once makes every post generic."
  - "Run the week, then keep the hooks that drew replies. Next week's spine is usually last week's best-performing angle said harder."
tips:
  - "Open the week with the angle most likely to get a reply, not the one you like most. A week that starts quiet stays quiet."
  - "One mistake post per week does more for trust than six how-to posts. People believe someone who has been wrong in public."
  - "Carousels are for the how and the list; text posts are for the take. Matching format to angle is half the battle."
  - "Do not delete a flat post and repost it later unchanged. Rewrite the hook, keep the body, and let it earn the slot."
example_output: |
  SPINE
  Most paid ads fail not because the creative is bad but because the offer
  underneath it is not worth buying.

  SEVEN ANGLES
  1. How: how to read an ad account in five minutes
  2. Mistake: the campaign I ran for six weeks before admitting the offer
     was the problem
  3. Proof: the landing page change that cut cost per lead by a third
  4. Unpopular take: creative testing is mostly procrastination
  5. Before/after: the same ad, two offers, two very different numbers
  6. Question: what is the most expensive lesson your ads taught you
  7. Tool: the three metrics I check before I touch anything

  DAY BY DAY
  Mon, text post, hook: "Your ads are not underperforming. Your offer is."
  Proves the spine in four lines.
  Tue, carousel, hook: "How to read an ad account in five minutes."
  Five slides, one metric each.
  Wed, story poll: "Blaming creative or blaming offer?" Feeds Friday.
  Thu, text post, hook: "I spent six weeks fixing the wrong thing."
  The mistake post, with the real timeline.
  Fri, short video, hook: "One number tells you if the offer works."
  The proof post, screen recording of the metric.
  Sat, text post, hook: "The most expensive lesson I've paid for."
  Before/after of the two offers.
  Sun, carousel, hook: "Three metrics before you touch anything."
  The tool list, kept short.

  PROOF CHAIN
  Thursday carries the six-week mistake. Friday carries the cost-per-lead
  number. Neither pretends the fix was instant.

  BACKUP HOOKS
  "I deleted a campaign that was 'working' and cut spend by a fifth."
  "Most people test creative when they should be testing price."

  ORDER
  Start Monday with the take. It is the sharpest claim of the seven and the
  one most likely to draw a disagreeing reply, and replies carry the week.
faq:
  - q: "What if my idea is not that controversial?"
    a: "It does not need to be controversial, only specific. A clear claim with a number behind it will out-perform a safe topic with no edge, and the plan will show you where to add the edge."
  - q: "Do I have to post every single day?"
    a: "No. Take the five strongest days and run them as a five-day week. Seven days of half-effort reads as noise; five posts people finish reading reads as a voice."
  - q: "Can I reuse these posts next month?"
    a: "Reuse the angles, not the captions. The spine stays the same idea said harder; every post gets a new hook and fresh proof, or the audience correctly decides you have run out of things to say."
  - q: "What if I do not have any numbers?"
    a: "Use honest measures: time saved, people helped, errors avoided, things you stopped doing. The plan marks any slot that needs proof you do not have yet, so you know exactly what to go and find."
