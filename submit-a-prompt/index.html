Submit a prompt

If you have built something that reliably beats the model's default behaviour, we want it. Send it to hello@f9xr.org.

What makes something worth publishing

The bar is high on purpose. The library is small because most prompt content is filler, and padding it back out would undo the point.

A submission is a strong candidate if it does at least one of these:

  • Fixes a specific, repeatable failure. Not “write better” - but “stops the model inventing a deadline I never gave it,” which is a real, common, expensive bug.
  • Forbids hallucination in a domain where it hurts. Research, legal, medical or financial drafting needs the model held on a short leash. Those prompts get priority.
  • Compresses a multi-step workflow into one pass. If it replaces four prompts and a manual cleanup step, that is worth documenting properly.
  • Degrades gracefully. A prompt that says “if my input is too thin, ask me the one thing you need and stop” is worth more than one that always produces output, because always producing output is the problem.

What we will decline

  • Anything that mostly restates the provider's own documentation.
  • Jailbreaks, filter bypasses, or prompts aimed at guardrails.
  • Thin rewordings of a prompt already in the library.
  • Anything promotional in disguise, or requiring an affiliate link.
  • Prompts whose only trick is length.

What to include

Send the prompt in full, plus enough context that someone else could run it cold:

  1. The prompt text itself, exactly as you use it.
  2. What it is for - the task, and what went wrong without it.
  3. Which models you tested it against, and roughly how often.
  4. One real example of input and output, with anything confidential removed.
  5. Its limits. Where it breaks down. This is the part most submissions omit and it is usually the most useful.

What happens next

A human reads every submission. You will get a reply either way, usually within a week, saying one of three things: published, published with edits, or declined with a reason.

We may rewrite for clarity or trim for length, and we will tell you if we did. Your name goes in the credits only if you want it to - we will ask, and “anonymous” is a fine answer.

By submitting you confirm the work is yours to share, and you agree to the terms - including that published prompts stay free to copy and use.
