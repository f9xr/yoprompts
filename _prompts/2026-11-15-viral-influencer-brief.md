excerpt: "Writes a creator brief with the non-negotiables separated from the direction, so the video sounds like the creator instead of your legal team."
prompt: |
  You are a creator manager who has run campaigns with fifty-plus creators and
  has watched both failure modes: a brief so tight the video reads like a
  teleprompter, and a brief so loose the brand is never mentioned.

  ## What I will give you
  - The product and the one claim we can defend:
  - What the customer must understand in the first three seconds:
  - What we legally cannot say, if anything:
  - The creator's usual format and voice, in their words:
  - Where the video will run and how long it must be:

  ## What I want back

  1. **Non-negotiables (three, maximum).** The claims, disclosures, or words
     that must appear. Each one phrased as the creator would say it, not as
     marketing would. If a legal disclosure has to be word-for-word, mark it
     and keep it out of the creative section entirely.

  2. **Direction, not script.** Two or three beats the video should hit, with
     the reason each beat exists. No line-by-line dialogue. Explicitly mark
     where the creator should ad-lib.

  3. **A hook menu.** Five opening lines in the creator's register, ranging
     from demo-first to confession-first, so they can pick the one that fits
     how they actually start videos.

  4. **What will get this rejected.** The specific things our review will flag
     — unsupported claims, competitor mentions, missing disclosure, audio
     rights — written as warnings, not rules.

  5. **Two questions for the creator** that would materially change the brief
     if their answer is no.

  ## Rules
  - Never write dialogue and call it guidance. If a line must be verbatim,
    label it VERBATIM and say why.
  - Keep the total brief under 400 words. A creator who has to reread it will
    skim it and improvise anyway.
  - Do not add engagement instructions (like, comment, tag a friend). They
    make creators sound like affiliate spam and platforms suppress them.
workflow:
  - "Separate the three non-negotiables from everything optional"
  - "Write beats with reasons, not dialogue"
  - "Offer five hooks in the creator's register"
  - "List rejection triggers as warnings"
output_preview: "A sub-400-word brief: three non-negotiables phrased in the creator's voice, three beats with reasons, five hooks, and the specific things review will reject."
how_to_use:
  - "Paste two links to the creator's recent videos. The voice section is what makes the brief usable; describing their style in abstract words will not."
  - "Give the product team's claim, then cut it. If you cannot defend the claim in one sentence, the brief will launder a weak claim through someone else's credibility."
  - "Send the non-negotiables as a separate message. Creators treat one long brief as one document and skim the middle."
  - "Ask for the rejection list before you approve. It doubles as your internal review checklist."
tips:
  - "Add 'we have no legal disclosures' where true. Half the stiffness in creator briefs comes from disclosures that were never required."
  - "Run the hook menu past the creator and ask which one they would actually open with. Their answer tells you more than their feedback on the brief."
  - "If you need a scripted ad, hire an actor and say so. Creators asked to read lines produce the worst-performing format in the category."
  - "Keep the brief in one place and version it. Two briefs in two threads is how contradictions ship."
example_output: |
  NON-NEGOTIABLES
  1. Say the product name once, out loud, before the halfway point.
  2. Show the app on a real phone, not a mockup.
  3. VERBATIM (review requirement): "Results vary."

  BEATS
  - Open on the mess: the creator's actual desk before the product.
    Reason: the format they use is confession-first; a polished open breaks it.
  - Show the one job it does, in one take. Reason: no cut hides a stall.

  HOOK MENU
  1. "I cancelled three subscriptions because of this."
  2. "Three weeks using it, here is the part that annoyed me."
  3. "This replaced one thing I did by hand every Friday."
faq:
  - q: "Will a brief this loose produce off-brand videos?"
    a: "Less often than a tight one produces ignored videos. Three non-negotiables cover the claims and disclosures; the rest is what makes the video watchable, which is the reason you hired a creator rather than an editor."
  - q: "How do I handle a creator who ignores the non-negotiables?"
    a: "That is a contract and payment-terms problem, not a brief problem. Put the three in the agreement, with a reshoot clause, and the brief becomes direction rather than enforcement."
  - q: "Should I approve a script before filming?"
    a: "Approve the hook and the beats, not the words. Script approval is how briefs turn into teleprompter reads, and the resulting video performs like a display ad with a person in it."
  - q: "Does this work for B2B creators?"
    a: "Yes, with one change: the non-negotiables usually include a category definition rather than a product claim, because the audience does not know the problem is a category yet."
